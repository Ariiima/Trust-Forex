Each signal is issued with four targets and fixed risk-to-reward values. The targets exist before the outcome, which is the only reason a result can be judged at all.

:::stats
TP1 | R:R 1:0.5 | Fixed before outcome
TP2 | R:R 1:1 | Fixed before outcome
TP3 | R:R 1:2 | Fixed before outcome
TP4 | R:R 1:3 | Fixed before outcome
:::

## Reached, target by target {.blue}

If TP2 is the highest target reached, the signal counts as **Reached** for TP1 and TP2. It does not count as Reached for TP3 or TP4. Each target keeps its own Win Rate, and the highest rate never stands in for all four.

| Target | R:R | Example signal |
|---|---|---|
| TP1 | 1:0.5 | Reached |
| TP2 | 1:1 | Reached |
| TP3 | 1:2 | Not Reached |
| TP4 | 1:3 | Not Reached |

:::callout info Not Reached is not Stop Loss
Not Reached only means the selected target was not reached. It does not say whether, or when, Stop Loss was touched.
:::

## Why the rule matters

A provider who decides after the outcome what counted can always rebuild the story. Fixing the targets first removes that option: the same basis applies to every signal, in strong periods and weak ones, and the record stays comparable over time.
